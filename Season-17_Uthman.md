### *The man of two lights — so modest the angels were shy of him, so generous his wealth fed an army and gave the ummah one Qur'an.*

# EPISODE 62 — THE EMPIRE EXPANDS

## The Story

When the Shura of six met after Umar's (R.A.) martyrdom, the choice came down to two of the greatest men alive: Uthman ibn Affan and Ali ibn Abi Talib (R.A.). Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf (R.A.) spent three sleepless days consulting the people of Madinah — men and women, elders and youth — and the community's voice settled on **Uthman**. Ali (R.A.) gave him his pledge. The third caliph took office in **23 AH / 644 CE** by genuine consultation.

Who was this man?

He was **Uthman ibn Affan**, called **Dhun-Nurayn** — "the Possessor of Two Lights" — because he had married two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ, one after the other (Ruqayyah, and after her death, Umm Kulthum). No one else in history holds that honour. He was among the very first to accept Islam, an early migrant to Abyssinia and then Madinah, a wealthy merchant who spent his wealth like water in the path of Allah.

And he was famous for one quality above all: **haya** — modesty, shyness, a bashful dignity so deep that the Prophet ﷺ said even the angels were shy in his presence. He was soft-spoken, gentle, generous to a fault, and so devoted to the Qur'an that he was known to complete its recitation in a single night of prayer.

His generosity was legendary even *before* he became caliph. When the Muslims first came to Madinah, the only sweet water well, **Bir Rumah (the Well of Rumah)**, was owned by a man who sold its water dear. The Prophet ﷺ asked who would buy it and give it freely to the Muslims, promising Paradise. Uthman bought it and made its water free for all. And when the Muslims set out for the hard expedition to **Tabuk** — the "**Army of Hardship** (Jaysh al-'Usrah)" — when the treasury was empty and the journey brutal, Uthman financed a huge portion of the army from his own pocket. The Prophet ﷺ said that day that nothing Uthman did after that could harm him.

Under his caliphate, the empire reached its widest extent yet. The conquests Umar had begun rolled onward: westward across **North Africa**; the navy he authorised took **Cyprus** (the first Muslim naval expedition — the believers, a desert people, now sailed the Mediterranean); and eastward the armies pushed into **Khorasan** and beyond, completing the absorption of the old Persian lands. For most of his twelve years, the empire was prosperous, stable, and vast.

He was a builder and an expander — but more than that, he was about to give the ummah a gift that would outlast every empire: a single, unified Qur'an. And the reason *why* he did it grew directly out of this very expansion.

## The Qur'an Says

- **Surah At-Tawbah (9:100)** — "*And the first forerunners among the Muhajirun and the Ansar... Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him...*" — Uthman was among the earliest of the forerunners (*as-Sabiqun al-Awwalun*). ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah Az-Zumar (39:9)** — "*...Is one who is devoutly obedient during periods of the night, prostrating and standing, fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord...?*" — early commentators connected this description to Uthman's nights of prayer and Qur'an. ✓ Qur'an / tafsir.

- **Surah Al-Hadid (57:10)** — "*...Not equal among you are those who spent before the conquest [of Makkah] and fought... Those are greater in degree...*" — Uthman's early spending and sacrifice place him among this higher rank. ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah At-Tawbah (9:117)** — "*Allah has already turned in mercy to the Prophet and the Muhajirun and the Ansar who followed him in the hour of difficulty (sa'at al-'usrah)...*" — the "hour of difficulty" is the very Tabuk expedition Uthman bankrolled. ✓ Qur'an.

## The Authentic Hadith

- **The angels' shyness:** The Prophet ﷺ said of Uthman, *"Should I not feel shy before a man before whom the angels feel shy?"* — **Sahih Muslim**. ✓ His haya was praised by revelation-grade testimony.

- **The Well of Rumah:** The Prophet ﷺ said, *"Whoever digs (buys) the well of Rumah, for him is Paradise."* — so Uthman bought it. Reported in **Sahih al-Bukhari** (and the detailed narration in at-Tirmidhi). ✓

- **The Army of Hardship:** The Prophet ﷺ said regarding Uthman's spending on the expedition of Tabuk, *"Nothing will harm Uthman after [what he has done] today."* — **Sunan at-Tirmidhi**, graded **hasan**. ✓

- **A man of Paradise, and a coming trial:** The Prophet ﷺ gave Uthman glad tidings of Paradise *together with the mention of a calamity (bala') that would befall him.* — **Sahih al-Bukhari** and **Sahih Muslim** (the famous narration of the well/garden, when Abu Musa guarded the gate and the Prophet ﷺ foretold each Companion's fate). ✓ — His martyrdom was foretold by the Prophet ﷺ himself.

- **Mountain of Uhud:** "*...a Prophet, a Siddiq, and two martyrs*" — the second of the "two martyrs" was Uthman (with Umar). **Sahih al-Bukhari**. ✓

## Historical / Theological Context

**Why "Dhun-Nurayn"?** Marrying two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ in succession (after each passed away) is an honour unique to Uthman in all of history — hence "the Possessor of Two Lights."

**The naval milestone:** The Cyprus expedition (~27–28 AH) marks the birth of Muslim sea power. For a people whose civilisation was rooted in the desert, building and sailing a navy across the Mediterranean was a remarkable leap — and it had been forbidden under Umar out of caution, then permitted under Uthman.

**Stability, then strain:** For roughly the first half of his rule, Uthman presided over peace and unprecedented prosperity. The strains that would later be exploited — complaints about the conduct of some appointed governors, and resentment in the new garrison cities far from Madinah — built up gradually in the second half. We will treat that with care in Episode 64.

**Sunni–Shia note (respectful):** Sunni tradition holds Uthman (R.A.) as the third Rightly-Guided Caliph, rightly chosen by the Shura, and a beloved Companion and son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ. Shia tradition maintains its distinct view of the succession (favouring Ali, R.A., from the outset) and is generally more critical of aspects of Uthman's administration. We present the Sunni position, acknowledge the Shia view exists, and direct no insult at anyone.

## Weak & Disputed Narrations

- ⚠️ Many of the later, detailed criticisms of Uthman's governors and policies come through **partisan chains** (some traced to agitators of the fitnah itself) and were amplified by later sources like at-Tabari's collected reports. Historians urge strong caution; not every "complaint" preserved is a verified fact.

- ⚠️ Specific dates and commanders for the North Africa, Cyprus, and Khorasan campaigns vary between sources; the broad expansion is certain, the granular details less so.

- ✗ Wholesale character-attacks on Uthman (R.A.) found in some polemical literature are rejected; they contradict the explicit, authentic praise of the Prophet ﷺ.

## What We Don't Know

- ❓ The precise sequence and scale of every campaign in his reign.
- ❓ The exact degree to which specific governors did or did not overstep — the sources are coloured by the later conflict.
- ❓ The full internal dynamics of the Shura's three days of deliberation.

## The Lessons

1. **Generosity is an investment with Allah, not a loss.** Uthman gave a well to a city and an army to a campaign — and the Prophet ﷺ guaranteed it would never harm him. What you give for Allah's sake is the only wealth that lasts.
2. **Modesty (haya) is strength, not weakness.** The mightiest ruler of his age was the most bashful man in the room — and the angels honoured him for it.
3. **Be a person of the Qur'an.** Uthman's heart was anchored in nightly recitation. It is no accident that *he* was the one entrusted to preserve it for all time.
4. **Prosperity is its own test.** The empire's wealth and reach grew enormously under Uthman — and with ease came the seeds of the trial. Comfort tests the ummah as surely as hardship does.

# EPISODE 63 — ONE BOOK FOR ONE UMMAH

## The Story

The empire had exploded outward — from Armenia to North Africa, from the edge of India to the islands of the sea. And with it travelled the Qur'an, carried in the hearts and on the tongues of the Companions and those they taught. But something began to go wrong — not with the Qur'an itself, but with the *people far from its source.*

The Qur'an had been revealed in **seven ahruf** — seven modes or dialectal readings — a mercy from Allah so that the various Arab tribes, with their different tongues, could all recite it. In Madinah, where the Companions lived side by side, everyone understood that these variations were all divinely sanctioned. But now there were vast new Muslim populations in **Iraq, Syria, Armenia, Egypt** — new converts learning from teachers who had learned different valid modes. To these newcomers, the differences were not a known mercy; they were a source of confusion. People began to say *my recitation is correct and yours is wrong.* Children grew up hearing their region's reading as the only one.

The man who saw the danger clearly was the Companion **Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman (R.A.)**. He had been on the campaigns in **Armenia and Azerbaijan**, where the fighters of Syria and the fighters of Iraq came together — and he heard them dispute about the recitation, each group certain the other was wrong. It alarmed him to his core. He saw a future in which the ummah might split over the very Book that was meant to unite it. He rode hard to Madinah, came before Uthman (R.A.), and said, in effect: *Save this ummah before they differ about their Book the way the Jews and Christians differed about theirs.*

Uthman understood at once. This was not a problem of a corrupted text — the Qur'an was perfectly memorised by thousands and written in the *suhuf* (sheets) that Abu Bakr (R.A.) had compiled after Yamama and that were now kept with **Hafsah bint Umar (R.A.)**, the Prophet's ﷺ widow. The problem was the *uncoordinated spread of different valid readings* and the disputes it bred among the unlearned.

So Uthman acted with the consensus of the Companions. He sent for Hafsah's *suhuf*. He appointed a committee led by **Zayd ibn Thabit (R.A.)** — the same trusted scribe who had compiled the Qur'an under Abu Bakr — together with several of the Quraysh. Their task: to produce a definitive set of master copies (**masahif**, sing. *mushaf*), written in the **dialect of the Quraysh** (the dialect in which the Prophet ﷺ himself recited), to serve as the unifying standard. They worked with extreme care; where they differed on how to write a word, they deferred to the Qurayshi tongue, since the Qur'an had been revealed in it.

When the masahif were finished, Uthman sent a master copy to each of the major centres — Makkah, Kufa, Basra, Syria, and others — along with a qualified reciter to teach from it. And he gave the order that the variant private manuscripts be respectfully retired, so that the ummah would rally around one written standard.

This is the **Uthmanic mushaf** — completed around **25–30 AH** — and it is, in a real sense, the Qur'an you hold in your hands today. Every printed copy on earth, from Indonesia to Morocco, traces back to this standardisation. Uthman did not change a single word of the Qur'an; he *unified the ummah around it* and closed the door to discord forever. It is one of the greatest services any human being has ever rendered to the revelation of God.

## The Qur'an Says

- **Surah Al-Hijr (15:9)** — "*Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur'an and indeed, We will be its guardian.*" — Allah's own guarantee of preservation; Uthman's work was a means through which that divine protection was realised. ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah Al-Qiyamah (75:17–18)** — "*Indeed, upon Us is its collection [in your heart] and [to make possible] its recitation. So when We have recited it [through Jibril], then follow its recitation.*" — the Qur'an's gathering and recitation are framed by Allah Himself. ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah Aal-e-Imran (3:103)** — "*And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided...*" — the exact danger Uthman was guarding against: division over the Book. ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah Fussilat (41:42)** — "*Falsehood cannot approach it from before it or from behind it; [it is] a revelation from a [Lord who is] Wise and Praiseworthy.*" — the Book that cannot be corrupted. ✓ Qur'an.

## The Authentic Hadith

- **The reason for the standardisation — Hudhayfah's alarm:** Anas ibn Malik (R.A.) reported that Hudhayfah came to Uthman, alarmed by the disputes over recitation among the fighters of Syria and Iraq during the Armenian/Azerbaijan campaigns, and urged him to unite the ummah on one mushaf — so Uthman ordered the copies made and distributed. — **Sahih al-Bukhari** (the foundational narration for the whole episode). ✓

- **The committee and the Qurayshi dialect:** In the same Bukhari narration, Uthman instructed Zayd ibn Thabit and his companions: *if you differ with Zayd about any of the Qur'an, then write it in the dialect of the Quraysh, for it was revealed in their tongue.* — **Sahih al-Bukhari**. ✓

- **The seven ahruf:** The Prophet ﷺ said, *"This Qur'an has been revealed in seven ahruf (modes), so recite whichever is easy for you."* — **Sahih al-Bukhari** and **Sahih Muslim**. ✓ (This explains the *origin* of the valid variations Uthman later unified the written standard around.)

- **Zayd ibn Thabit's trust:** Zayd was the Prophet's ﷺ chief scribe of revelation, and it was he whom both Abu Bakr and Uthman entrusted with the Qur'an — a continuity confirmed in **Sahih al-Bukhari**. ✓

- **Uthman the son-in-law:** That he married two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ is established; the Prophet ﷺ honoured him deeply — consistent with the authentic narrations across **Bukhari** and **Muslim**. ✓

## Historical / Theological Context

**This is the crucial point most people misunderstand:** Uthman did **not** "edit," "censor," or "change" the Qur'an. The text was already perfectly preserved — memorised by huge numbers of *huffaz* and written in Abu Bakr's *suhuf*. What Uthman did was (1) produce authoritative master copies in one dialect, and (2) standardise the written *script* across the empire to end disputes among the newly-converted who didn't realise the variant readings were all valid. The companions of the era — including Ali ibn Abi Talib (R.A.) — *endorsed* this. Ali (R.A.) is authentically reported to have said that Uthman acted only with the consultation of the Companions, and that had he himself been in charge, he would have done the same. ✓ (reported in the works of the scholars; reflects the consensus that this was a praiseworthy act, not a controversial one).

**Why it mattered so much:** The Qur'an is the constitution of the ummah. A fracturing of its written transmission, in an empire spanning three continents, could have produced regional "versions" and permanent division — exactly what happened to earlier scriptures. Uthman's standardisation slammed that door shut. **This is why the Qur'an today is identical in Indonesia, Nigeria, and Saudi Arabia.**

**Timing (the deliberate fix in this season):** Note the order of events — the **expansion came first** (Episode 62). It was precisely the *recitation differences that surfaced during the expansion's campaigns* (Hudhayfah on the Armenian front) that *prompted* the standardisation. The mushaf is a fruit of the conquests' challenges.

**Sunni–Shia note (respectful):** Both mainstream Sunni and mainstream Shia scholarship affirm the integrity and completeness of the Qur'an as preserved — the same Qur'an is recited by both. Sunni tradition credits Uthman's standardisation as a great service. We present that, and note no mainstream school claims the text was altered.

## Weak & Disputed Narrations

- ✗ Polemical claims (from fringe sources, ancient and modern) that Uthman "removed verses" or "censored" the Qur'an are **false and rejected** by mainstream scholarship of both major traditions. They contradict the mass-transmitted (*mutawatir*) preservation of the text.

- ⚠️ The precise number of master copies Uthman commissioned (reports range from four to seven or more) and exactly which cities received them vary between sources. The fact of distribution to the major centres is certain; the exact count is not.

- ⚠️ Some detailed sub-narratives about a single missing/last verse located with a particular Companion are authentic in their core (Bukhari) but get embellished in retelling; stick to the Bukhari wording.

## What We Don't Know

- ❓ The exact year the project concluded (somewhere in the ~25–30 AH range) and its precise duration.
- ❓ The fate of every retired manuscript and the full membership of the writing committee beyond the named figures.
- ❓ Whether any of the original Uthmanic masahif survive today — several manuscripts are *claimed* as Uthmanic, but definitive attribution is debated by specialists. ❓/⚠️

## The Lessons

1. **Unity around the truth is worth guarding fiercely.** Uthman saw a crack forming in the ummah's foundation and moved decisively to seal it. Some problems must be solved *before* they explode.
2. **Allah preserves His Book — and He uses people to do it.** Divine protection (15:9) worked *through* the courage of Hudhayfah, the care of Zayd, and the resolve of Uthman. We are invited to be instruments of what Allah has already promised.
3. **Difference can be mercy or menace — knowledge decides which.** The seven ahruf were a mercy when understood and a fitnah when not. Ignorance turns even gifts into conflict; knowledge restores them to blessings.
4. **The greatest legacy is service to revelation.** Empires rose and fell; the mushaf Uthman unified still opens in a billion homes every dawn. Invest your life in what outlives you.

# EPISODE 64 — THE SIEGE & MARTYRDOM

## The Story

The second half of Uthman's (R.A.) reign darkened slowly, the way a clear sky clouds before a storm.

The empire was vast and rich, but distance breeds discontent. In the far garrison cities — especially **Kufa, Basra, and Fustat (Egypt)** — grievances accumulated and were stoked. Some were sincere complaints: that certain governors (several of them Uthman's relatives, appointed for their competence and loyalty but resented by others) had been heavy-handed, or that the new wealth was not distributed as people wished. Some grievances were exaggerated, and some were manufactured outright by agitators who *wanted* fitnah. The reports of this period are notoriously tangled, and a careful student must hold them loosely.

Uthman's response was the response of a gentle, God-fearing man: he met complaints with patience, sent investigators, replaced governors when shown cause, and tried to absorb the anger rather than crush it. He would not unleash the sword on his own people over disputes. But to the agitators, mercy looked like weakness, and patience looked like an opening.

In **35 AH / 656 CE**, bands of armed agitators from the garrison cities converged on Madinah under the pretext of presenting grievances. What followed is one of the most painful episodes in Islamic history: they laid **siege to the caliph's house.** They cut off his access — even, by the well-known reports, his access to water — to a man who had once given a whole city its water for free. The leading Companions were horrified; sons of the great Companions, including (by various reports) the sons of Ali, Talha, and Zubayr (R.A.), stood guard at his door to protect him.

And here is the heart of the story, the thing that defines Uthman (R.A.) forever: **he refused to let his own blood become a cause for civil war.** He had the men and the authority to order the agitators crushed. He chose not to. He restrained those who would have fought for him, because he would not be the reason Muslim blood was shed in the streets of the Prophet's ﷺ city. He had a sound memory of the Prophet ﷺ foretelling his trial, and he faced it as a believer faces a destined test — fasting, praying, reading the Qur'an.

The agitators eventually broke in. On a day in **Dhul-Hijjah 35 AH**, Uthman ibn Affan (R.A.) — Dhun-Nurayn, the man of two lights, the son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ twice over, the man who gave the ummah its Qur'an — was struck down **while the mushaf was open before him, reciting the Book of Allah.** It is reported that drops of his blood fell upon the very words of the Qur'an he had spent his life serving. His wife Na'ila (R.A.) tried to shield him and lost fingers to the blade. He died a martyr, exactly as the Prophet ﷺ had foretold on the slopes of Uhud.

The ummah was never the same. With his murder, the door of fitnah — the great trial — swung fully open, and it would not close for a long time. The blood of the third caliph would cast its shadow over everything that came next.

## The Qur'an Says

- **Surah Al-Baqarah (2:137)** — "*...so Allah will suffice you against them, and He is the Hearing, the Knowing.*" — This very ayah, by the famous report, was the verse open before Uthman when he was struck, and his blood is said to have fallen upon it. ✓ Qur'an (the ayah); ✓/⚠️ on the precise detail (widely transmitted, see below).

- **Surah Aal-e-Imran (3:169)** — "*And never think of those who have been killed in the cause of Allah as dead. Rather, they are alive with their Lord...*" — the rank of the martyr that Uthman attained. ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah Al-An'am (6:151)** — "*...and do not kill the soul which Allah has forbidden except by right...*" — the gravity of unlawful killing, especially of a believer, and most especially a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ. ✓ Qur'an.

- **Surah Al-Anfal (8:25)** — "*And fear a trial (fitnah) which will not strike those who have wronged among you exclusively...*" — a chilling warning about fitnah that engulfs the wider community, fulfilled in this era. ✓ Qur'an.

## The Authentic Hadith

- **His martyrdom foretold (the garden/well narration):** The Prophet ﷺ, with Abu Musa al-Ash'ari (R.A.) guarding the gate, foretold Paradise for Abu Bakr, Umar, and then Uthman — *"...with a trial (bala') that will befall him."* — **Sahih al-Bukhari** and **Sahih Muslim**. ✓ His murder was prophesied.

- **"Be patient, for you will come to me":** In a sound narration, the Prophet ﷺ told Uthman that Allah may clothe him in a "shirt" (the caliphate), and if the hypocrites sought to remove it, he should not take it off — i.e., not abandon his post — and be patient until he meets the Prophet ﷺ. — recorded in **Sunan at-Tirmidhi** and **Ibn Majah**, graded **hasan/sahih** by scholars. ✓ This explains *why* Uthman refused to fight or flee: he understood it as the Prophet's ﷺ counsel.

- **Uhud's testimony:** "*...a Prophet, a Siddiq, and two martyrs*" — Uthman the second martyr. **Sahih al-Bukhari**. ✓

- **The Prophet's ﷺ love and Uthman's modesty:** "*Should I not feel shy before a man before whom the angels feel shy?*" — **Sahih Muslim**. ✓ (Contextualises the dignity of the man so brutally besieged.)

## Historical / Theological Context

**The debated figure of Abdullah ibn Saba:** Many traditional accounts blame a shadowy convert named **Abdullah ibn Saba** for orchestrating the agitation against Uthman and sowing the seeds of later sectarianism. **Honest scholarship requires caution here.** The figure is reported heavily through **Sayf ibn Umar**, a narrator whom hadith critics graded as **weak (da'if)** and unreliable. Some historians therefore treat the Ibn Saba narrative with great suspicion or consider his role greatly exaggerated or even largely legendary; others hold that a real agitator by that name existed but that the elaborate conspiracy attributed to him is overstated. **The responsible position: a fitnah of competing agitators is certain; the precise role of "Ibn Saba" is genuinely debated and rests on weak chains.** ⚠️/❓ We name the dispute and do not build doctrine on it.

**Who were the rebels?** They were factions from the garrison cities — *not* the senior Companions, who almost universally condemned the murder. It is critical to distinguish the leading Companions (who tried to protect Uthman) from the agitating mob (who killed him). Conflating the two is a historical and moral error that has fuelled centuries of sectarian polemic.

**Why this is "the first fitnah":** Uthman's murder was the hinge of early Islamic history. It went unpunished (the killers melted back into the factions and could not be cleanly identified or apprehended), and the demand for *qisas* (just retribution) for his blood became the fault-line of the conflicts that followed in Ali's (R.A.) caliphate — the Battle of the Camel and Siffin. That is the bridge into Season 18.

**Sunni–Shia note (respectful):** Sunni tradition unanimously regards Uthman (R.A.) as a wrongfully-martyred Rightly-Guided Caliph and reveres him. Shia tradition holds a more critical view of his administration and the grievances against him, while *also* generally condemning the lawless manner of his killing. The two traditions weigh the politics differently; both should recoil from the murder of a Companion. We present the Sunni position, note the difference, and **curse no one.**

## Weak & Disputed Narrations

- ⚠️ The entire **Abdullah ibn Saba** complex rests largely on the weak narrator **Sayf ibn Umar** — flagged above. Treat as disputed.

- ⚠️ Lurid, blow-by-blow accounts of the assault, the specific named attackers, and exact dialogue inside the house come through **partisan and often weak chains** (especially via at-Tabari's collected reports). The *core* — siege, deprivation of water, his refusal to fight, his murder while reciting the Qur'an, his wife's injury — is well-attested; the granular details are not all reliable.

- ⚠️ Reports assigning specific blame to named senior figures for the murder are **disputed and often polemical**; the senior Companions are reported to have tried to *protect* him. Do not assign blame to Companions based on weak partisan reports.

- ✗ Sectarian narratives that celebrate or justify the killing are rejected outright.

## What We Don't Know

- ❓ The exact identities and number of the killers — they were never cleanly identified or brought to justice, which is itself central to the tragedy that followed.
- ❓ The true extent of any coordinating "conspiracy" (the Ibn Saba question) versus a convergence of independent grievances.
- ❓ The precise final words and exact verse open before him — the *Al-Baqarah 2:137* report is widely transmitted and beloved, but the absolute certainty of the detail is beyond strict proof. ⚠️
- ❓ How much of the governors' alleged misconduct was real versus manufactured grievance.

## The Lessons

1. **Mercy under pressure is not weakness — it is the costliest courage.** Uthman could have ordered the sword drawn to save his own life. He chose to die rather than let Muslim blood flow in the Prophet's ﷺ city. Few have ever been that brave.
2. **Fitnah feeds on rumour and grievance.** The trial grew in the dark soil of exaggeration, distance, and stoked resentment. Guard your tongue and verify before you accuse — especially when believers are involved.
3. **Hold your post with patience when Allah tests you.** Uthman remembered the Prophet's ﷺ counsel and did not abandon his trust, facing the storm with the Qur'an open before him.
4. **Be careful how you speak of the Companions.** This episode has been weaponised for sectarian hatred for fourteen centuries. The believer's adab is to honour them all, distinguish the noble from the mob, and refuse to make a Companion's grave a platform for cursing.

## ✓ SEASON 17 COMPLETE

**What we witnessed:** The reign of Uthman Dhun-Nurayn (R.A.) — the bashful, generous man of two lights. Under him the empire reached its widest span, the Muslims first took to the sea, and, most enduringly, the ummah received **one unified Qur'an** — standardised in response to the recitation disputes the expansion itself had exposed, preserving the Book identically for every generation since. And then came the darkening: a fitnah of grievance and agitation, a siege, and the martyrdom of a Companion who chose to die rather than shed his people's blood — exactly as the Prophet ﷺ had foretold.
